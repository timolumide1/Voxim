# Lesson plan: Phases of the Moon
Grade 7 Science · Earth & Space unit · 50 minutes
Standard: MS-ESS1-1 (develop and use a model of the Earth-Sun-Moon system to describe the cyclic patterns of lunar phases)

## Objectives
Students will be able to:
1. Explain that the Moon does not make its own light; it reflects light from the Sun.
2. Explain that half of the Moon is always lit, and the phase we see depends on how much of that lit half faces Earth.
3. Put the eight phases in order and say that a full cycle takes about 29.5 days.

## Vocabulary
phase, waxing, waning, crescent, gibbous, lunar cycle

## Bell ringer (5 min)
On a sticky note, draw what the Moon looked like the last time you saw it. Stick it on the board. We'll come back to these at the end.

## Direct instruction (12 min)
- Ask: does the Moon make its own light? Take a few answers.
- The Moon is like a mirror for sunlight. It has no light of its own.
- Key point: the Sun ALWAYS lights half of the Moon, the half facing the Sun. Students find this hard because we never see the whole lit half except at full moon.
- As the Moon orbits Earth, we see different amounts of the lit half. That's the phase.
- MISCONCEPTION to address head-on: many students think Earth's shadow causes the phases. It doesn't. Earth's shadow only falls on the Moon during a lunar eclipse, which is rare. If the shadow caused phases, we'd never see a crescent next to the Sun in the sky.
- Order: new moon, waxing crescent, first quarter, waxing gibbous, full moon, waning gibbous, third quarter, waning crescent, then new again. About 29.5 days.
- Waxing = the lit part is growing. Waning = it's shrinking. Memory trick: "waxing" like a candle getting built up.

## Activity: lamp and ball model (20 min)
Groups of 3. Materials: one lamp per room (lights off), a styrofoam ball on a pencil for each student.
Student holds the ball at arm's length and turns slowly in a circle (counter-clockwise), watching the lit part of the ball change. Partners record what they see at 8 positions on the worksheet.
Teacher circulates; check that students keep the ball slightly above their head so their head's shadow doesn't block it (otherwise they "make" an eclipse).

## Check for understanding (5 min)
If tonight is a first quarter moon, what will it look like in about one week? (Answer: full moon.)
Why don't we see the whole lit half at a crescent? 

## Closure (5 min)
Go back to the bell ringer sticky notes. Sort them into the phase order on the board.

## Exit ticket
In one or two sentences: why does the Moon appear to change shape?

## Homework
Moon journal: sketch the Moon each night for a week, note the date and time.
