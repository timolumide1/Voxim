# Phases of the Moon: visual plan
Class: Grade 7 science, 50 min · Standard MS-ESS1-1 · Built from: teacher-notes.md

| # | Lesson part | Clip? | Why | What the viewer sees | Length | Plays |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Bell ringer: sketch the Moon | no | Students' own ideas first, before any explaining | — | — | — |
| 2 | Does the Moon make its own light? | **yes: MirrorLight** | Light's path is invisible; the "mirror" idea needs to be seen | Sunlight travels to the Moon, bounces off and reaches an eye on Earth | ~0:30 | After taking students' answers |
| 3 | Half the Moon is always lit | **yes: HalfLit** | The idea students find hardest (teacher's note); it's spatial | A Moon lit from one side; then the Moon circling Earth with its lit half always facing the Sun | ~0:40 | Straight after clip 2 |
| 4 | Orbit → the phase we see | **yes: TwoViews** | Needs two viewpoints at once: from above, and from Earth | Left: the Moon orbiting, seen from above. Right: what we see from Earth, changing with it, with the phase name and day count | ~1:20 | After the "half is lit" point, before the activity |
| 5 | Misconception: Earth's shadow | **yes: Shadow** | Teacher wants it addressed head-on; a picture shows where the shadow really is | The wrong idea crossed out; Earth's shadow pointing away from the Sun, a quarter moon nowhere near it | ~0:45 | Right after clip 4 |
| 6 | Order of phases, waxing/waning, 29.5 days | **yes: Sequence** | Eight shapes in order are seen faster than read | The eight phases in a row, named, with waxing and waning spans and the 29.5-day cycle | ~0:45 | End of direct instruction; again at closure |
| 7 | Lamp and ball activity | no | A hands-on model the students make themselves; a video would give away what they should discover | — | — | — |
| 8 | Check for understanding | **yes: Check** | A pause card the teacher can stop on; the answer is revealed after | Question, timer, then the full moon and why | ~0:25 | After the activity |
| 9 | Closure, exit ticket, homework | no | Class routines and students' own writing | — | — | — |
